# Security Systems & Low-Voltage Licensure — New Jersey

A four-year professional program combining the technical competence to design and install
alarm, camera, and low-voltage systems with the regulatory path to being licensed to do it
for money in New Jersey — covering the burglar alarm license, the fire alarm license, and
the telecommunications wiring exemption.

Part of [Roura-io Academy](../README.md). Verified against the New Jersey Administrative
Code on **13 August 2026**.

> **This program is not legal advice and does not confer any license, credential, or
> academic credit.** New Jersey's Division of Consumer Affairs is the only authority on
> what it requires. Rules change; fees change; providers lose approval. Every requirement
> below carries a citation so you can check it yourself, and you should, before spending
> money on any of it.

## Credential map — which one covers which work

New Jersey does not issue one "low-voltage license." It issues several narrow credentials
from **two different boards**, and the work you have described doing — networking installs,
cameras, alarms — crosses at least two of them. This table is the orientation for the
whole program:

| The work | Credential you need | Issued by |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Voice/data cabling within a premises (structured cabling, network drops, Wi-Fi APs) | **Limited Telecommunications Wiring Exemption** | Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors |
| Burglar alarm / electronic security installation and service | **Burglar Alarm license** | Fire Alarm, Burglar Alarm and Locksmith Advisory Committee |
| Fire alarm installation and service | **Fire Alarm license** *(or NICET Level II — see below)* | Same Committee / NJ DCA Division of Fire Safety |
| Locks, safes, access hardware | **Locksmith license** | Same Committee |
| Any electrical work at 10 volts or more outside the above | **Electrical Contractor license** | Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors |
| Residential work of any kind for a homeowner | **Home Improvement Contractor registration** | Division of Consumer Affairs |

Three consequences worth internalizing before you plan anything:

1. **The telecom exemption does not cover alarm work.** N.J.A.C. 13:31-4.1 states the
   exemption does not apply to the work of an electrical contractor, **alarm business
   work, or locksmithing**. Running network cable under the exemption and then installing
   a burglar alarm on the same visit requires the alarm license too. These are additive,
   not alternative.
2. **There is no "low voltage license."** The Board is explicit that it has no such
   category. Work under 10 volts is exempt from the business permit; everything at or
   above 10 volts needs either the electrical contractor license or one of the narrow
   exemptions above.
3. **The electrical contractor license is a different order of commitment** — a four-year
   US Department of Labor approved apprenticeship plus one additional journeyman year
   before you may even sit the exam, which is a three-part test covering trade, alarm
   systems, and business and law. Do not plan around it casually; plan around the
   exemption and the alarm license instead unless you intend to become an electrician.

> **Not the same thing:** a New Jersey "security license" under SORA (the Security Officer
> Registration Act) is for security *guards* — people, not systems. It is unrelated to
> alarm installation. If someone tells you that you need SORA to install cameras, they are
> confusing two different regimes.

## Read this before you buy a course

The single most important fact about New Jersey alarm licensure is not the 80 hours of
classroom training. It is this:

> "at least four years of practical hands-on experience, which shall include a minimum of
> **6,720 hours**, working with tools in the installation, alteration, or repair of wiring
> for fire alarms, burglar alarms, and/or electronic security systems"
> — [N.J.A.C. 13:31A-3.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-31A-3-1)

The 80-hour course is the *small* requirement. It is roughly two weeks of study. The
experience requirement is roughly **three and a quarter years of full-time work**, and no
course, package, or provider can shorten it. Any vendor whose marketing implies the
80 hours is the path to a license is selling you the last mile of a marathon.

This program is therefore built to run **alongside employment that accrues those hours**,
not instead of it. The four-year shape is not arbitrary pacing — it is the statutory clock.

### The three qualification pathways

N.J.A.C. 13:31A-3.1 gives three routes. Pick yours before Term 1, because it determines
how many hours you need to log:

| Pathway | Prior education | Hands-on experience required |
| :-- | :-- | :--: |
| **A — Experience** | High school diploma or equivalency | 4 years / **6,720 hours** |
| **B — Engineering degree** | Bachelor's in **electrical engineering** | 1 year / **1,680 hours** |
| **C — Trade school** | Minimum one-year course in trade-related electronics | 3 years / **5,040 hours** |

All three additionally require the 80 hours of technical courses, a high school diploma or
equivalency, being at least 18, good moral character, no first-, second-, or third-degree
criminal convictions in the 10 years prior to application, fingerprinting, and passing the
applicable examination.

> ⚠️ **The Roura-io Academy [Computer Science program](../computer-science) does not
> satisfy Pathway B.** That pathway requires an accredited bachelor's degree in electrical
> engineering. This academy is not accredited and grants no degree. Pathway C's "one-year
> course in the study of trade-related electronics" is a plausible fit for an accredited
> trade-school program — **not** for self-study, and not for this repository. If you want
> Pathway B or C, you are enrolling somewhere that issues a transcript. Otherwise you are
> on Pathway A, and this program assumes Pathway A.

### The 80 hours, itemized

Per N.J.A.C. 13:31A-3.1, the 80 hours must include:

| Hours | Subject | Citation |
| :--: | :-- | :-- |
| 2 | Barrier Free Subcode | N.J.A.C. 5:23-7 |
| 2 | New Jersey Uniform Construction Code (excluding Barrier Free) | N.J.A.C. 5:23 |
| 2 | Americans with Disabilities Act Code | 36 C.F.R. |
| 2 | Industrial safety | — |
| 2 | New Jersey law and rules | — |
| 70 | Trade-related subjects in the field of licensure | — |
| **80** | **Total** | |

## Term structure

Eight terms of roughly 15 weeks. The academic load is deliberately light — 6–8 hours per
week — because the load-bearing work this program is scheduled around is the day job that
generates your 6,720 hours.

### Term 1 — Electrical and electronic fundamentals

You cannot install what you don't understand. Before any vendor platform, learn what a
volt is.

Resource | Source | Focus
:-- | :--: | :--
[All About Circuits — Textbook](https://www.allaboutcircuits.com/textbook/) | EETech | DC, AC, semiconductors, digital
[Khan Academy — Electrical Engineering](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/electrical-engineering) | Khan Academy | Circuit analysis fundamentals
[NEC low-voltage articles (Art. 725, 760, 770, 800)](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards) | NFPA | Class 2/3 circuits, fire alarm circuits, communications
[OSHA 10-Hour Construction](https://www.osha.gov/training/outreach/construction) | OSHA | Jobsite safety (paid card, ~$80)

NFPA codes are free to read online with a registered account. You will not be able to
practice in this field without reading them, and reading the actual code beats reading
someone's summary of it.

**Term deliverable.** A wiring and testing notebook: schematics you drew, measurements you
took with a multimeter, and one written explanation of a Class 2 circuit and why the
distinction matters for what you're allowed to run in a wall.

### Term 2 — Structured cabling and physical installation

Resource | Source | Focus
:-- | :--: | :--

[TIA-568 family overview](https://tiaonline.org/) | TIA | Commercial building cabling standards

**Term deliverable.** A documented cable run: pathway, fire-stopping, labeling scheme,

### Term 3 — Alarm systems: fire

[NFPA 70 — National Electrical Code](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards) | NFPA | Article 760 in depth
[NJ Uniform Construction Code](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/) | NJ DCA | The code you actually build to in this state

**Term deliverable.** A fire alarm design for a small commercial space: device layout,

### Term 4 — Alarm systems: intrusion, access, and video

Resource | Source | Focus
:-- | :--: | :--

### Term 7 — Business formation and the application

Resource | Source | Focus
:-- | :--: | :--
[Business.NJ.gov](https://business.nj.gov/) | State of NJ | Entity formation, tax registration, employer obligations

**Term deliverable.** A complete business package: entity registered, insurance quotes in

> **The near-term unlock.** The telecommunications wiring exemption and HIC registration
> have **no 6,720-hour prerequisite** — they are business credentials, not experience-gated
> licenses. You can be legally registered to do residential data cabling long before the
> alarm license is attainable. If the goal is revenue while the experience clock runs, this
> term is the one to pull forward to the front of the program.

audit. And the **Home Improvement Practices Act** dictates required contract terms for
residential work; a contract missing them is a regulatory problem regardless of how well
the install went.

###### Manufacturer and platform training
Most vendors gate their training behind a dealer account, which you get after you have a
licensed business. Plan for it after Term 7, not before: Ubiquiti, Axis, Alarm.com,
Resideo/Honeywell, DSC, and Qolsys all run their own programs, and dealer certification is
frequently a condition of buying equipment at trade pricing at all.

### Term 8 — Licensure and capstone

By now the experience hours should be logged or nearly so. This term is the application
itself plus a capstone that proves competence independent of the paperwork.

1. Complete the 80-hour approved course.
2. Register in the state licensing portal and submit the application.
3. Complete fingerprinting through the State's designated vendor and clear the criminal
   history review.
4. Sit the examination once your approval-to-test is issued.
5. On licensure, secure the business license insurance and bond (below) and register the
   business.

**Capstone.** A complete system design and installation package for a real site: intrusion,
video, access, and network; code citations; device schedules; wiring diagrams; battery and
PoE calculations; a commissioning test report; an as-built package; a customer handover
document; and a maintenance plan. If someone who has never seen the site can operate and
service it from your documents, you're done.

## What the business itself requires

Separate from your personal license, per
[N.J.A.C. 13:31A-3.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-31A-3-5),
an alarm **business** licensee must carry:

- **General liability insurance of $1,000,000**, and
- **Insurance coverage or a surety bond in favor of the State of New Jersey of $10,000**

The same rule requires maintaining an in-state office (or filing a power of attorney),
marking vehicles with the business identification, and operating a 24-hour emergency
service line. The business is responsible for its employees' conduct.

### Employing people

Per [N.J.A.C. 13:31A-3.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-31A-3-6),
employees must be of good moral character. An employee may work **unsupervised** only with
three years of practical experience plus 38 hours of training (2 Barrier Free, 2 UCC,
2 ADA, 2 industrial safety, 32 trade-related) — or by passing a competency examination
administered by the Committee. Plan your crew structure around this before you hire.

### Home Improvement Contractor registration

Residential work is separately regulated. HIC registration requires an application,
a certificate of liability insurance of at least **$500,000 per occurrence**, and a **$110**
fee, filed with the Division of Consumer Affairs' Home Improvement Unit. Sources disagree
on whether the cycle is annual or two-year — confirm at
[njconsumeraffairs.gov/hic](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/hic/Pages/applications.aspx).

### Telecommunications wiring exemption

This is the credential that covers the network-cabling half of your work, governed by
[N.J.A.C. 13:31-4.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-31-4-1)
under [N.J.S.A. 45:5A-18](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-45/section-45-5a-18/).

**What it covers.** Wiring within a premises, inside or outside a building, for voice
and/or data transmission at voltages compatible with the system being installed and
connected to an FCC-recognized communication network — including interconnection of data
wiring between computers and terminals.

**What it does not cover.** Electrical contracting, alarm business work, and locksmithing.
It also specifically does not permit service conductors, feeder or branch circuit work,
telecommunications wiring to power-operated controlled equipment, or work in hazardous
(classified) locations under NEC Article 500.

**To apply**, per the rule you must certify that you are:

- familiar with and in full compliance with **Part 68 of the FCC regulations**
  (47 C.F.R. § 68.1 et seq.);
- familiar with **NEC Article 800** and responsible for obtaining local permits;
- not performing prohibited work without the proper license; and
- not subcontracting covered work to unlicensed entities.

A processing fee applies under N.J.A.C. 13:31-1.6, a personal interview may be required,
and the Board issues an identification card on approval. Address changes must be reported
within 10 days; ownership or name changes require 30 days' notice and return of the card.
Application:
[Application for Telecommunications Wiring Exemption](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/elec/Applications/Application-for-Telecommunications-Wiring-Exemption.pdf) ·
Board: (973) 504-6410.

**Study this before applying** — the certifications are substantive, not formalities:
FCC Part 68, NEC Article 800, and the Uniform Construction Code permit process are the
Term 1, Term 2, and Term 6 material in this program.

### Fire alarm — two routes

Fire alarm work has a second path that the burglar alarm side does not. A Fire Alarm
Systems Contractor may hold either a **NICET Level II Fire Alarm Systems certification**
or a fire alarm business license under P.L. 1997, c. 305. If fire alarm is a business line
you want, compare the NICET route against the Committee license before committing to the
80-hour path for that class — the requirements, cost, and timeline differ. Start with the
[NJ DCA Division of Fire Safety contractor certification](https://www.nj.gov/dca/divisions/dfs/)
requirements.

### The electrical boundary

Where low-voltage alarm work ends and licensed electrical contracting begins is set by the
[Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/elec/Pages/default.aspx),
not by voltage intuition. Confirm the boundary for any job that involves line-voltage
work, panel connections, or new circuits. Getting this wrong is the most common way a
competent installer ends up with a complaint against them.
